## Overview

Preparing for product-based tech interviews gets significantly easier when you know *how* you will be evaluated in every round. This repository organizes Flipkart's role-specific interview preparation guides across four core tracks—covering screening stages, hands-on coding and modelling rounds, system architecture, portfolio reviews, and culture fitment.

Whether you are gearing up for a 120-minute Machine Coding marathon or structuring a Data Science presentation, use the quick matrix below to jump straight to your track.

## Role-by-Role Interview Breakdown

Click any track below to expand its complete round-by-round structure, durations, and focus areas.

<details>
<summary><strong>1. Software Development Engineer (SDE)</strong> — <em>7 Rounds across 2 Phases</em></summary>

<br>

**Phase 1: Exploratory & Screening**
* **Round 1.1 — Exploratory Discussion (30 mins):** Conversation with a potential Hiring Manager covering your background, eligibility, career aspirations, and an overview of the role.
* **Round 1.2 — Technical Phone Screening (60 mins):** Technical evaluation covering problem-solving fundamentals and product thinking before moving to onsite rounds.

**Phase 2: Onsite Interviews**
* **Round 2.1 — Machine Coding (120 mins):** Split into three structured segments:
  * *Pre-Coding (15 mins):* Clarify requirements and align on expectations.
  * *Coding (90 mins):* Build a working, modular solution with periodic checkpoints.
  * *Post-Coding (15 mins):* Live demo of your program walking through expected outputs and edge cases.
* **Round 2.2 — Problem Solving & Data Structures (60 mins):** Evaluation of algorithmic thinking, data structures, and time/space complexity analysis.
* **Round 2.3 — Design (60 mins):** Object-Oriented Design (e.g., *Design a Parking Lot*) or System Design focusing on requirement gathering, clean abstractions, and extensibility.
* **Round 2.4 — Team Fitment (60 mins):** Deep dive into your strengths, collaboration style, and overall alignment with the team.
* **Round 2.5 — Culture Fitment (60 mins):** Behavioral and hypothetical scenarios assessing alignment with Flipkart's core values.

**Core Topics to Brush Up:** Proficiency in your primary language (Java, C++, Python, etc.), Big-O complexity, Object-Oriented Design principles, and distributed systems fundamentals.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>2. Senior Software Development Engineer (Sr. SDE)</strong> — <em>7 Rounds across 2 Phases</em></summary>

<br>

**Phase 1: Exploratory & Screening**
* **Round 1.1 — Exploratory Discussion (30 mins):** Hiring Manager conversation to align on role scope, past ownership, and hiring process expectations.
* **Round 1.2 — Technical Phone Screening (60 mins):** Technical depth check prior to the onsite loop.

**Phase 2: Onsite Interviews**
* **Round 2.1 — Machine Coding (120 mins):** Follows the 15-min Pre-Coding, 90-min Coding, and 15-min Post-Coding format—with heightened focus on readability, correctness, clean interfaces, avoiding tight coupling, entity responsibilities, and data flow validation.
* **Round 2.2 — Problem Solving & Data Structures (120 mins):** Extended deep dive into complex algorithmic problems and optimized implementations.
* **Round 2.3 — Design & Architecture (90 mins):** Dual focus on:
  * *Low-Level Design (LLD):* Logical view, process view, entity modeling, and API design.
  * *High-Level Design (HLD):* Distributed components (Load Balancers, Queues, etc.), trade-offs, and scalability across multiple components.
* **Round 2.4 — Team Fitment (60 mins):** Assessment of technical leadership, strengths, growth areas, and role alignment.
* **Round 2.5 — Culture Fitment (60 mins):** Behavioral evaluation grounded in Flipkart's leadership and cultural values.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>3. Data Scientist</strong> — <em>8 Rounds across 2 Phases</em></summary>

<br>

**Phase 1: Exploratory & Screening**
* **Round 1.1 — Exploratory Discussion (30 mins):** Hiring Manager call covering eligibility, aspirations, and the type of applied data science work at Flipkart.
* **Round 1.2 — Technical Phone Screening (60 mins):** Focused primarily on past projects, technical depth, and applied work experience.

**Phase 2: Onsite Interviews**
* **Round 2.1 — Presentation Round (60 mins):** Present a past data science project or research problem (an abstract is shared in advance) detailing your exact contributions and design decisions.
* **Round 2.2 — Hands-on Skills (120 mins):** Practical problem-solving using a provided dataset and problem statement—testing end-to-end exploratory analysis, feature engineering, and modelling.
* **Round 2.3 — Depth in Data Science (60 mins):** Translating open-ended, unseen business problems into formal ML/modelling frameworks and choosing the right evaluation metrics.
* **Round 2.4 — Depth in Mathematical Modelling (60 mins):** Rigorous discussion on the mathematical foundations behind algorithms, along with your ability to tweak or adapt models to novel constraints.
* **Round 2.5 — Team Fitment (60 mins):** Discussion with potential managers on strengths, impact, and team alignment.
* **Round 2.6 — Culture Fitment (60 mins):** Scenario-based evaluation of mindset, collaboration, and values.

**Core Topics to Brush Up:** Bias-variance tradeoff, cross-validation protocols, evaluation metrics, supervised learning, clustering, and probabilistic graphical models (e.g., Dirichlet models).

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>4. User Researcher (UXR)</strong> — <em>6–8 Rounds across 2 Phases</em></summary>

<br>

**Phase 1: Exploratory & Screening**
* **Round 1.1 — Exploratory Discussion (30 mins):** Hiring Manager discussion outlining the team, role expectations, and detailed hiring stages.
* **Round 1.2 — Technical Phone Screening (60 mins):** Review of past research experience and product thinking (may include an offline exercise before advancing to onsite).

**Phase 2: Onsite & Portfolio Evaluations**
* **Round 2.1 — Research Portfolio Presentation (60 mins — Researcher 1 to Lead):** Walkthrough of past studies emphasizing storytelling, research rigor, speed of delivery, and stakeholder management.
* **Round 2.2 — Research Planning & Scoping Exercise (60 mins — All Levels):** Interactive exercise (whiteboarding, scenario building, design thinking) assessing how you clarify ambiguous problem statements and design research plans tied to business goals.
* **Round 2.3 & 2.4 — Domain Understanding 01 & 02 (60 mins each — Directors & Above):** Deep dives with PMs/stakeholders covering multi-method research, desirability, user delight, and domain problem framing (e.g., ticket booking systems).
* **Round 2.5 — Team Fitment (60 mins):** Role alignment and collaboration assessment.
* **Round 2.6 — Culture Fitment (60 mins):** Values and cultural alignment discussion.

</details>

## Machine Coding & Design Playbook

> [!TIP]
> **Treat the first 15 minutes as your highest-leverage time.** In both Machine Coding and Design rounds, interviewers evaluate how thoroughly you clarify requirements and edge cases *before* writing code or drawing architecture boxes.

1. **Prioritize a Working, Demo-Ready Core:** In Machine Coding (120 mins), always ship a clean, runnable MVP first before layering on bonus features.
2. **Design for Extensibility, Not Over-Engineering:** Separate responsibilities cleanly across classes/interfaces, avoid tight coupling, and be ready to explain how your design handles a sudden requirement change.
3. **Narrate Your Trade-offs:** Whether optimizing an algorithm, picking an ML evaluation metric, or choosing a UX research method, articulate *why* you picked approach A over approach B.
4. **Know Your Past Work Inside Out:** Expect deep follow-ups on every architecture choice, mathematical assumption, or stakeholder conflict from projects listed on your resume.

## Culture & Fitment Essentials

Flipkart places strong weight on intent, ownership, and cultural alignment across all levels. Prepare concrete **STAR-format** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) stories—including at least one candid reflection on a past failure—mapped to core values highlighted in the guides:

* **Customer First:** Decisions anchored in real user pain points and long-term customer trust.
* **Bias for Action:** Moving decisively under ambiguity without waiting for perfect conditions.
* **Integrity:** Transparent communication, ownership of mistakes, and ethical decision-making.
